鬼の田 (Oni no Ta)

A Heian-era farming simulator where a bored war deity tries to learn rice cultivation, and the player decides whether overwhelming power becomes restraint, instability or destruction.

PC / Console2D top-downCDD + Systems Document
POWER VS RESTRAINT

High Concept

Oni no Ta (“The Demon’s Field”) combines cozy agricultural routines with a destabilizing Divine Energy system. Farming acts as both mechanic and metaphor: ordinary labor gives the player a way to practice restraint, while boredom and power create pressure toward catastrophic release.

Core Loop

Explore village
→
Interact with villagers & Jojo
→
Farm rice & manage irrigation
→
Manage Divine Energy + Boredom
→
World reacts to behavior

System Relationships

Divine Energy

Power use raises instability. High levels mutate crops, corrupt soil and trigger curse events.

Boredom

Repeated mundane farming builds boredom; when full, energy begins leaking automatically and Rampage Mode can unlock.

Farming

Tile-based rice planting, irrigation, stamina and seasonal cycles create the peaceful rhythm the player is tempted to break.

Relationships

NPCs respond to patterns of restraint, dialogue and farming behavior rather than a single morality button.

Rampage

A 60-second destruction sequence radically changes audio and visuals, reframing combat as loss of control rather than mastery.

Choice & Progression

No conventional fail state: behavior pushes the world toward peaceful farmer, sealed deity or dominant dark-god outcomes.

Relationship Design

The systems document proposes a hidden social matrix tracking Trust, Respect, Fear and Attachment. NPCs evaluate longer-term patterns, including power usage, consistency, farming behavior and responses to stress, so village reactions can progress from friendliness to distance, rumors, avoidance and finally calling exorcists.

Jojo, the protagonist’s daughter, is designed as the emotional anchor. Her response mirrors the player’s alignment and can influence escalation, story branches and endings.

Progression Through Consequence

The economy is intentionally not centered on currency. Crops, time, effort and social trust are the meaningful resources. Likewise, progression is behavioral rather than level-based: the world, villagers and ending change according to how much restraint the player demonstrates.
